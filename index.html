Four Pillars of EMISC 2027

Moving beyond individual clinical training towards institutional sustainability, regional capacity, and safe laparoscopic infrastructure.

01

Universal Basic Laparoscopic Training

Every surgeon, surgical fellow, and resident must attain accredited basic laparoscopic training at least once. We champion standardized foundational simulation and dry-lab competencies across all surgical staff, ensuring a safe, consistent baseline before entering the operating room.

02

Advanced Hands-on Laparoscopic Training

Bringing together experienced surgeons, surgical educators, and trainees to explore advanced laparoscopic techniques, emerging surgical technologies, and innovative approaches to minimally invasive surgery. Through expert-led masterclasses, live demonstrations, simulation-based sessions, and hands-on workshops, we aim to facilitate knowledge exchange, refine surgical skills, and advance technical excellence in laparoscopic surgery across Africa.

03

Optimized MIS Access & Patient Rights

Minimally invasive surgery must be systematically optimized across every surgical specialty and teaching referral center. We uphold that every patient has an essential right to access minimally invasive surgery, benefiting from tissue-sparing precision, faster recovery, and lower surgical morbidity.

04

Inter-Hospital & Inter-Institutional Collaboration

Strengthening inter-organizational partnerships and hospital collaboration within Ethiopia while extending linkages across Africa to advance health equity and modern surgical access. Uniting institutions nationwide and regionally to facilitate shared surgical training, faculty exchanges, resource pooling, and collective innovation.

Our Mission

Expanding Minimally Invasive Surgery in LMICs

MIS Africa was founded by a coalition of close surgeon friends united by a shared vision: to expand access to minimally invasive surgery across low- and middle-income countries, starting with Ethiopia.

“Our mission is to elevate healthcare quality by advancing structured MIS training, simulation, and evidence-based surgical mentorship.”

To date, MIS Africa has equipped several teaching hospitals and trained over 100 surgeons, surgical residents, OR nurses, and biomedical engineers, contributing to sustainable patient outcomes and surgical dignity.

Historical Milestone

EMIS 2026 Recap

In April 2026, MIS Africa hosted the groundbreaking 1st Ethiopian Minimally Invasive Surgery Conference under the theme “Safe Approach, Safe Surgery: Expanding Minimally Invasive Services in Ethiopia for Better Health Outcomes.”

Keynote Speaker • EMISC 2026

Dr. David Jeffcoach, MD, FACS

Associate Clinical Professor of Surgery – UCSF Fresno • Former Chair of Surgery, PAACS Soddo Christian Hospital

Dr. Jeffcoach is a trauma and critical care surgeon with global leadership in trauma systems and laparoscopic simulation training. Serving for four years in Ethiopia, he helped establish foundational MIS programs and residency education across the country.

Meet the leadership driving EMIS 2027 forward

Dr. Efeson Thomas

MD, FACS, FCS(ECSA) • General, Colorectal & Minimally Invasive Surgery

MCM-CSH (Korea Hospital)

"Minimally Invasive Surgery (MIS) is not a luxury reserved for affluent patients. It is an essential component of modern surgical care that should be accessible to everyone who needs it."

Considering its many advantages over conventional open surgery, MIS may be even more beneficial to patients in Low- and Middle-Income Countries (LMICs), where reducing surgical trauma, shortening hospital stays, facilitating faster recovery, and minimizing the economic burden of illness can make a significant difference.

In Africa, access to MIS should not be determined by a patient's income or geographical location. MIS Africa exists to bridge the gap in access to minimally invasive surgery across the continent — promoting its availability, affordability, and sustainability across all surgical specialties.

Our vision: Making MIS accessible and affordable for every patient who needs it, across Africa.
